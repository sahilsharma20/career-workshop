Why this matters for competitive exams

How Springfield's ICSE curriculum builds an early advantage

Springfield follows the ICSE curriculum, which goes deeper into most subjects from Class 9 onward. Here's what that looks like in practice, and how it feeds into JEE/NEET/Olympiad preparation.

Subject depth (Classes 9–10)A broader syllabus that covers more topics in detail, building wider subject knowledge earlier
Science practicalsSeparate, structured practicals from Class 9, so lab-based thinking starts early
Assessment styleLonger-format, application-based questions that reward genuine understanding over recall

Springfield's view: the depth and application-based habits this builds from Class 9 onward tend to transfer well once students start JEE/NEET/Olympiad preparation. Aakash's teachers work with our students to connect that foundation directly to each exam's own syllabus and pattern. This is our own read based on our students' experience.

About the workshop

A Sunday morning built around real decisions, not a sales pitch

Choosing a stream, an entrance exam, or even whether to start coaching at all is a decision most families make with more worry than information. This workshop exists to close that gap — for the student sitting the exam, and for the parent signing the cheque.

Aakash Institute's career counselling team will lead a group session for students of Classes 7, 8, 9 and 10, covering JEE, NEET, Government Olympiads and Indian Olympiad exams — how each actually works and how to plan for them without losing the rest of school life in the process.

Alongside it, and open to every family who books ahead, is a free one-to-one counselling slot — a private conversation about one child's specific situation, not a general talk. Details on booking one are in the section below.
